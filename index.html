The problem

The industry sells sessions.
Sessions don’t develop players.

Somewhere between the drills, the drive home, and next week’s session, development is supposed to happen. It doesn’t — because nobody coaches the 166 hours.

01

The Hour Myth

1–2 coached hours out of 168. The rest are unmanaged — so unmanaged that no published study even measures between-session work in youth sports. The layer where development happens is the layer nobody owns.

02

The Black Box

Parents spend $1,000–$2,300 a year and can’t see development happening. Cost is the #1 reason families quit — but “not worth it” is what invisibility sounds like.

03

The Plateau Machine

Reps without feedback don’t transfer. Drills that look productive in practice disappear in games. Solo work without design plateaus by design.

“Few service sectors ask customers to invest so much money and time while accepting so little visibility in return.”

— Matt Young, youth sports researcher

04

The Motivation Trap

70% of kids quit organized sports by 13. Shame-based accountability — public leaderboards, pressure — accelerates it. Effort needs autonomy, visible progress, and a coach who’s actually there.

05

The 90-Day Cliff

26% of new training clients never book a second session. Retention isn’t a moment — it’s a slow, invisible drift starting month two, when a parent silently asks: “Is this actually working?”

Why it works

Built on sports science, not vibes.

Bandwidth feedback

Motivation by design

Load-managed
